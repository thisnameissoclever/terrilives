# Wilted daisy logo review

Owner rejection recorded on October 17, 2026. The [rejected logo pair](wilted-daisy-light-dark-rejected.png)
is retained as evidence of a failed visual review. It must not be used as an
approved site asset or favicon source.

The owner's brief required coherent foreground and background petal
attachments, a subdued ochre center with its brown speckles retained, a visibly
missing petal, and a more spent flower. The selected serif lettering and
sickly ivory dark-theme lettering were to remain.

An adversarial visual reviewer rejected the previous revision for an
inconsistent petal attachment, a smooth bright center, and a full petal skirt.
The first replacement established coherent attachments and a missing-petal
gap. Review then identified an overly thick center and excessive discoloration
at the petal tips. The composed revision flattened that center and reduced the
tip treatment.

The reviewer accepted the pair after checking petal layering, the missing-petal
gap, speckles, and mood. That acceptance was wrong. The owner rejected the
oversized, projecting center, which still read as a peanut-shaped growth. The
review had assessed improvement in other details without resolving this
repeated objection. Reducing the dark sidewall had not corrected the silhouette.

The retained file is byte-identical to the reviewed output. Its SHA-256 digest
is `ef65854b688cb95562f4a231bb420ce85e3e259c0d05a1dbf7056e178d8b4454`.

The replacement must change the center's geometry and proportion, not merely
its shading. An independent review must reject this example for the owner's
stated reason before judging the replacement. Transparent exports, favicon
sizes, app icons, and site integration remain unverified.

## Replacement with a front-facing flower

The [replacement light and dark pair](wilted-daisy-front-facing-light-dark.png)
uses a newly drawn flower head. A fresh adversarial reviewer first rejected the
retained example for its projecting silhouette. The replacement was reviewed
as an isolated flower and then again in the exact composed pair.

In the replacement, the small round ochre disk sits inside the petal face. It
has no visible sidewall or projecting lobe. Brown specks remain. The open wedge
between the upper-left and lower-left petals conveys the missing petal, while
the bowed stem and curled dry tips retain the spent appearance. Both the main
assistant and fresh reviewer accepted the replacement for these visual
criteria. Owner acceptance and production exports are separate decisions.

On October 18, the owner rejected the changed facing direction and selected
the earlier logo for a local overlap correction. See the
[October 18 review](2026-10-18-petal-overlap.md) for the resulting artifact.
