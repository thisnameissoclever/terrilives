# Fitted bathing loop, 2026-10-06

Base revision: `2f319c3bb14b4c798505e1b6c75fa3186f6f4822` (main after the toilet batch).
This record covers the seated bathing loop only. The shower remains a separate

## Source pose

The earlier floor-recline family (prototypes 01 to 03, retained under
`assets/models/bathroom/actions/review/bath/`) could not find upper-back
support and pushed the torso through the basin floor. The accepted pose comes
from the wall-backed solve the retained reports called for:

1. `bath_wall_support.py` derives the inner head-end wall plane from the
   shell's two flat facets (pitch 17.2 degrees from vertical, inward normal
   toward the taps) and solves one recline angle for pelvis, back and head
   equal to that pitch, plus one translation. `test_bath_wall_support.py`
   was red before the helper existed.
2. `bath_wall_backed.py` applies that frame, bends the knees with the feet
   forward on the basin floor and rests the hands on the thighs, then settles
   the whole body until the seat rests one millimetre above the floor and the
   rearmost surface sits one millimetre off the wall. Seat support is measured
   on the actual downward-facing pelvis surface near the floor; wall support
   is measured with rays along the wall normal in the wall's own tangent
   frame. Both certificates are complete 7 by 9 grids at 6 mm spacing.
3. The opaque water surface sits at 52 cm, 5 cm below the rim, inset from the
   actual basin contour; it is declared a non-solid occluder. The declared
   bathing appearance from the shower work applies: the existing warm skin
   material on the torso and sleeves, thirteen garment details not rendered,
   no new anatomy, head and hair unchanged. Clearance is measured on the full
   54-object body against all twelve fixture solids.
4. Two nearest-normal containment hits on the open trouser tubes are reviewed
   by ray parity along six axes (zero crossings in every direction, so the
   upward ray meets no surface at all) and recorded as open-mesh artifacts
   rather than contacts: one on the basin floor under the left trouser leg
   at (-0.140, -0.682, 0.150) m, one at the shell's rim edge beside the
   right trouser leg at (0.249, -0.919, 0.562) m. The producer's rim
   constant is 0.56 m, one centimetre below the 0.57 m profile rim.

Prototype 10 was reviewed by an independent fresh-context reviewer, who
accepted the pose for a loop and export and asked for two proof-writer
corrections (validate the fixture after resetting the facing rotation, and
set the water flag) and a wider containment review; prototypes 11 and 12
carry those corrections with the same joint targets to within 1e-9 metres.
Prototype 12 is the accepted source: `review/bath/prototype-12-wall-backed-water/`,
with its editable model, four green views, source snapshots and proof. The
superseded wall-backed prototypes 05 to 11 keep their proofs, renders and
source snapshots but not their editable models, which carried the same or a
rejected frame and would have added about 200 MB to the repository. The
first loop receipt (`loop-01`) was discarded unrecorded because its closure
inventory mistakenly listed the always-hidden book and eyelid props; the
renderer now limits that inventory to the 54 body objects, and `loop-02` is
the accepted batch.

## Loop, export and import

The quiet loop `bath_loop_v1` nods only the head, up to three degrees, over
four samples and sixteen ticks; every other bone is re-checked against the
accepted targets each sample. The loop renderer re-measures seat support,
wall support and complete clearance on every sample with the thirteen hidden
details shown, then renders four facings, four frames and four owners for the
single bathing appearance plus sixteen body-ink passes at the original
1280 by 1408 canvas.

`bath_export_contract.py` and `export_bath_loop.py` validate that receipt and
encode the 320 by 352 scene layers with the shared additive encoder. Matching
hashes prove nothing about the evidence inside a receipt, so the contract
re-derives every certificate from its own witnesses instead of trusting the
recorded flags. Every grid cell must be a distinct point on the recorded
6 mm lattice forming an actual surface pair; seat cells must lie on the
basin floor profile and seat witnesses on the actual floor at 0.15 m; wall
cells must be ray pairs along the accepted plane's normal, expressed in its
tangent frame with no tangential drift between wall and body points, and
wall witnesses must lie on that plane. Each support patch is recomputed
from witness cells that must equal grid cells, must contain every grid cell
inside its bounds on consecutive lattice steps, and must reproduce the
receipt's area, extent, sample count and gap extrema; contact is decided by
the patch's own smallest gap, so a stray cell elsewhere on the grid cannot
supply it. The reviewed containment hits must equal the accepted source's
two hits on the trouser tubes against the shell, each with even crossings
on all six axes. Every static bone must equal the accepted pose within
1e-7 m; the head must keep its joint and length and its tail must equal the
accepted tail turned forward about the side axis by the loop's declared
angle for that frame, within 1e-6 m. The omitted garment details and skin
assignments must equal the declared bathing appearance; the body-ink stroke
inventory must equal the rendered inventory; and the fixture and water
digests must be identical on every frame of a facing.
`test_bath_export_contract.py` holds the forgery suite: collinear witnesses
with passed flags, a sparse Cartesian subset of the patch, witnesses moved
off the grid or carrying altered wall points, padded, duplicated,
off-lattice or lifted grid cells, a lifted patch beside a stray zero-gap
cell, swapped seat and wall certificates, an extra or missing reviewed hit,
a hit on the head or a tap handle, a head nod of the right size about the
wrong axis or backwards, a lengthened head with a zeroed length error,
moved static bones, wrong omitted details, moved fixture digests and a
mismatched ink inventory. The scene anchor applies the 21-pixel tile drop
and equals the empty tub's anchor `[80.00000953674316, 144.00043869018555]`.
The importer serves every shirt variant from the same sixteen scenes and
refuses an occupied scene whose anchor differs from its empty fixture.

## Limits carried to the next batch

The accepted receipts pin the hash of `bath_wall_backed.py`, so the producer
is unchanged in this batch and two of its weaknesses are enforced only by the
import contract: the producer excuses a containment hit when any single axis
shows even parity, while the contract requires all six axes even (both
recorded hits have zero crossings on every axis); and the producer's
`containment_review` keeps an unused nearest-distance variable. The loop
receipt's `contacts` and `reopened_contacts` rows are identical dictionaries,
so they do not by themselves show that the reopened model was re-measured;
the next loop batch should record each pass's model digest with its
measurement. Fixing either in the producer requires re-rendering the
prototype, loop and ink receipts.

Three further limits rest on the producer rather than the contract. The
contract checks the recorded cells for self-consistency and geometric
plausibility, not for having been measured: a fully fabricated block of
lattice cells inside the basin, or a whole wall grid slid along the plane
with its frame coordinates recomputed, would pass, so the measurement
itself rests on the pinned producer and the accepted source's independent
visual review. The contract verifies every render file's hash but nothing
binds a file's pixels to its facing and frame label; the GPU proof, the
played check and the visual review cover that binding. The loop receipt also lists the
toilet producer scripts among its inputs because the renderer pins the
whole actions directory, so editing a toilet script invalidates the bath
receipt until the loop is re-rendered; the extra inputs add constraints and
open no forgery route.

Source review and local runtime proof do not establish public deployment.
