# Family: who these people are to each other

[B-family-relationships] in `FEATURES.md`. The household is four people who each hold a relationship score toward the others, and nothing says that one of them is anybody's brother. A score can say they get on; it cannot say they are family, it cannot survive somebody moving out, and it cannot be inherited. The game needs kinship as authored data.

The whole item is large: a kinship graph, a family tree to look at, relatives outside the household, bereavement and inheritance, age rules, and autonomy that knows a parent from a stranger. This document splits it, and the first slice is the one that makes the rest possible.

## [FM-tie] A tie is a fact between two people

A tie joins two household members and says what the first is to the second: **partner**, **parent**, **child**, **sibling**. Four to begin with, appended in that order, because each is a different rule later: a parent ages differently from a partner, a sibling inherits differently from a child.

A tie is symmetric in storage and asymmetric in reading. It is stored once, from the lower of the two people to the higher, with the relation as seen from the lower; the reverse is derived, so parent and child are one stored fact read from either end. That is what keeps a tree consistent when somebody is added or removed, and it is why this is not two rows.

## [FM-identity] A tie names the person, not the slot

A tie names the two people by SimId, the permanent id every sim is issued once and never loses. It never names the entity index, the slot number of the entity carrying the person, because a slot is handed to somebody else once its entity is gone, and a tie that followed the slot would transfer to a stranger. That is the reason `Relationships` keys on SimId, and it is what lets a tie outlive somebody leaving the house or dying.

A command still names the two people by entity index, because that is the handle the page holds for somebody alive now, and the drain turns both into SimIds before anything is stored. The page reads ties back as SimIds and matches them against each member's SimId.

The first build of this slice keyed ties on entity index. Its saves still load: the envelope keeps that list, now always written empty, and appends the SimId list after it. A save that carries the old list is read by turning each index into the SimId of the sim the load rebuilt at it, which is exact because a load rebuilds every index as it was saved.

## [FM-save] Ties are saved, appended last

The save envelope gains a list of ties, each the lower of the two people's SimIds, the higher, and the relation code. Sorted, so two houses with the same family save identically, and appended last for the reason the floors list was, so every save written before ties existed still loads with nobody related to anybody.

The loader refuses a tie naming a SimId this world never issued, a sim tied to itself, a repeated pair, an out-of-order pair, or a relation code the game does not have. A SimId that was issued is accepted even when nobody in the house holds it now, because a tie outlives the person being here ([FM-identity]). It also refuses a save carrying ties in both the old entity-index list and the SimId list, since no build writes both.

## [FM-choose] A newcomer arrives as somebody

The New housemate form's second page gains one question: who this person is to the household. The choices are the four relations and "no relation", and the relation is to one named member, chosen from the household. "No relation" is the default. The member list is empty and disabled until a relation is chosen, and holds only the people who live here, so a relation chosen with nobody to attach it to simply sends no tie.

That is the whole of the first slice's player-facing surface: one question, one tie, saved.

## [FM-show] The panel says what they are

The selected person's relationship list already names every other member and how they feel about them. Each row that has a tie also says the tie, from the selected person's side: "Bill, her brother". The word is plain, and it comes from the relation and nothing else, so a sibling is a brother or a sister only when the game knows enough to say so; until it does, the word is "sibling".

## Slices

1. **[FM-slice-tie]**, this one: the tie, the save, the question on the form, and the word in the panel.
2. **[FM-slice-tree]**: a family tree to look at, built from the ties.
3. **[FM-slice-outside]**: relatives who do not live here, which needs sims the household does not hold.
4. **[FM-slice-consequence]**: autonomy and social weight that read the tie, and the bereavement and inheritance hooks, which wait on [B-death].

Ages and genetics are not in any of these and are not designed here.
