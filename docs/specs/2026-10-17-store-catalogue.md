# The store catalogue: previews and filters

Issue 252 defines this change. The Buy tool's three lists ([BM-shell] in `2026-09-21-buy-mode.md`, [CB-filter] in `2026-09-22-catalogue-browsing.md`) become a visual catalogue: every item for sale is a card with a picture of the item, and the filters are rows of buttons the player can clear with one control. The purchase itself, the ghost, the rotation, the colourway, the buying facts and the keys are unchanged.

## Intended behavior

In Build mode's Buy tool the player sees a grid of cards, one per item for sale: the item's picture as it is drawn in the game, its type, its model name and its price. A card the household cannot afford is grayed out. Pressing a card chooses the item, as choosing it from the old list did: the ghost appears, the buying facts show beneath the grid, and Buy, the rotations and Cancel appear beside the ghost. Two rows of buttons narrow the grid, by room and by the need an item is good for, and Clear filters shows everything again. On a phone the grid is a strip the player scrolls sideways inside the Build panel.

## Rules

1. **Cards.** The catalogue shows one card per item in the order the old list used (type name, then model name, then content order). A card shows the item's picture, the type name as its primary line, the model name as its secondary line when the item has one, and the price as the Funds line writes it. A card is a button named `{type}: {model} ({price})` (or `{type} ({price})` without a model name) for assistive technology; the price is in brackets because some model names hold a comma. The chosen item's card carries `aria-pressed="true"`. An item the household cannot afford has a grayed card marked `aria-disabled`, its picture dimmed more than its lines so the lines stay readable: it stays visible and reachable by keyboard so the player can see what to save for, and a press on it does nothing. Pressing the chosen card again keeps its ghost and facing.
2. **Pictures.** A card's picture is the item's base-facing sprite as the game draws it, cropped to its visible pixels (not to the content bounds the atlas records, which for some seats cover the whole canvas so a seated person fits), scaled to fit a canvas of twice its 72 pixel displayed size with smoothing, as the game filters its sprites, with its foreground layer drawn over it aligned by the sprites' anchors (a sprite without an anchor is anchored at its bottom center, as the renderer anchors it), in the art as drawn (colourway 0). The sprite comes from `purchasePreview` at tile (0, 0) and the base facing, the same query the ghost uses. The pictures are drawn once, the first time the Buy tool is shown: the pages of the sprite atlas are fetched one at a time, each decoded once, its layers cut down to their visible pixels, and released before the next is fetched, so a phone never holds two pages; the cards are drawn from the cut-outs once every page is read. A page that fails to load, or a layer that fails to be cut out or drawn, leaves each card it touches with a blank picture beside its name and price, never half a picture; the other cards are still drawn, and nothing else changes.
3. **Filters.** The Room row holds Every room and then each room any item belongs to, in alphabetical order with the room's words capitalized; the Show row holds Everything and then each need some item serves, in need order. One button of each row is pressed at a time (`aria-pressed`). Clear filters presses Every room and Everything; it is disabled while both are pressed, and when it disables itself its focus moves to Every room. The filter rules of [CB-filter] hold: a filter that hides the chosen item drops the choice and its ghost, the filters are kept while the tool is left and re-entered, and a Load presses Every room and Everything.
4. **Chosen item.** Beneath the grid, when an item is chosen, the panel shows what it showed before: the identity disclosure with the type, the model and its description, the Colour list, the facing, the price, what the item is good for and the buying facts. Nothing about an item is shown there, the Colour list included, when nothing is chosen; the status line and the hint stay.
5. **Keys and focus.** `[` and `]` step through the visible, affordable cards in grid order, as they stepped through the list. Choose item beside the ghost focuses the chosen card, grayed or not, or the first visible card when nothing is chosen. One card is in the Tab order: the card focus is on, else the chosen card, else the card last focused, else the first visible card. Inside the grid the arrow keys without modifiers move focus between visible cards in reading order, Home and End to the ends, and any card that takes focus, by key, click or Tab, becomes the Tab stop; Enter or Space press the focused card; Tab leaves the grid. Every control is disabled while a purchase is on its way or another pause holds, as the lists were.
6. **Phone layout.** In the Build dock the grid is one row of cards that scrolls sideways, each card laying a 52 pixel picture beside its type, model and price so the whole card fits the dock's short view; above it each filter row is likewise one sideways row of buttons. Cards are at least 44 pixels tall. On a desktop the grid wraps to as many columns as the panel's width holds and only the panel scrolls, with no second scroll area inside it.
7. **Strings.** Room, Every room, Show, Everything, Clear filters, and the card names above; everything else keeps its current wording.

## What this does not do

No search box, no price filter, no sorting control, no category filter (categories stay hidden during play per the writing skill), no picture of a colourway other than the art as drawn, and no picture of the item at another facing. The Furniture tool's own list is unchanged.

## Verification

Unit tests cover the card order and names, the pressed states, the affordability greying, the two filter rows and Clear filters, the drop of a hidden choice, the disabling while a purchase is on its way, Choose item's focus target and the arrow keys. A real-runtime test checks that every shipped item's card names a sprite the atlas has. The picture drawing is checked by hand in the played pass, with the pictures compared against the placed objects, and the pass also shows the phone strip and the Clear filters control. The Build controls proof script drives the grid instead of the list.
