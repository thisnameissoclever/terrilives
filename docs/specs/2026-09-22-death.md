# Death: the game is called Natural Causes

[B-death] in `FEATURES.md`. At design time nobody could die. The title promises otherwise, the ghost layer waits on it, and the family ties that just shipped have no bereavement to hook into. This document splits the item and defines the first slice.

The whole of it is large: aging and causes that are not aging, warnings a player can act on, preventable and unavoidable deaths, the body and the ghost, what it does to the household and to everybody's relationships, inheritance, memorial state, and the content controls a player needs to say "not that kind of game, thank you". None of that fits one increment.

## [DE-cause] One cause, and it is the player's to prevent

The first slice has exactly one way to die: **deprivation**, meaning energy or hunger held at zero. A sim whose energy or hunger has sat at zero for `death_after_ticks` (`content/tuning.toml`) in a row dies. Nothing else kills anybody: no age, no fire, no accident, no illness.

Deprivation is not the glossary's **neglect**, which is any need below 15 draining life satisfaction. Neglect is where deprivation starts, but a sim can be neglected for days without dying; only energy or hunger reaching zero and staying there counts.

Deprivation is the right first cause because it is the only one the player can already see coming. The needs panel shows the bar, the mood shows the misery, and the warning below is a third telling. A death the player could not have prevented is a different design problem and waits for a later slice.

## [DE-count] How long a need has been at zero

No existing number says how long a need has been at zero: a need clamps there, so a sim one tick into empty and a sim six hours into empty read the same. That is why exhaustion keeps its own counter (the comment above `exhaustion_energy` in `content/tuning.toml` explains it).

So each living sim carries a deprivation count: ticks in a row with energy or hunger at zero, back to zero the first tick neither is. The count is simulation state, in the world hash, and saved, appended to the save envelope like every list before it. It runs whether or not death is turned on, so switching death on part way through a crisis starts from the truth rather than from zero.

## [DE-warn] A warning that is a fact, not a hint

When death is on and a sim's deprivation count reaches `death_after_ticks - death_warning_ticks`, the sim's row says so plainly: their name, what is at zero, and that they will die if it stays there. The wording is functional and not a joke, as an error message is: this is the surface where humour would cost the most.

The warning itself is derived from the count and never stored. When death is off, no warning appears, because it would not be true.

## [DE-consequence] What a death leaves

- **The household loses them.** They stop being selectable, their needs and mood stop, and the roster no longer lists them.
- **Their ties stay.** A dead parent is still a parent ([FM-tie]); ties name people by SimId ([FM-identity]), so a tie survives its person leaving the world.
- **Everybody who knew them feels it.** Each surviving member's relationship toward them is kept, and a grief moodlet lands on every member of the household, its size and duration scaled by their affinity at the time of death. Affinity at or below the authored hatred cutoff produces no grief and no happiness bonus. A neutral acquaintance produces a small, short loss; a close friend produces a stronger, longer one.
- **Grief fades with time since the death.** Moodlets are derived from the world rather than stored, and grief adds time-dependent fading. Grief follows the same rule: its size is worked out from the tick the sim died, which the death record holds, falling to nothing over a duration between `grief_min_ticks` and `grief_ticks`, derived from the preserved affinity. Relationships toward the dead no longer decay. Nothing per survivor is stored.
- **Their things stay theirs until inheritance exists.** Furniture they bought stays where it is and keeps working. Nothing is sold, moved or reassigned by this slice.
- **The save carries it.** A dead sim is saved as a death record (SimId, name, tick of death, cause, and the SimId allocator boundary at death) rather than deleted, because a deleted sim is one whose ties, grief and memorial have nowhere to live, and because a save must not silently lose a person.

The identity boundary excludes housemates who arrive after the death from its grief. The tick is the in-game timestamp. This refinement was requested by the owner on 2026-09-29.

## [DE-remove] Taking a sim out of the running world

At design time nothing could remove a character; this is [F-entity-lifecycle] in `docs/GAME-SYSTEMS.md`, and death is its first user. The first slice takes the dead sim's entity out the way a sale takes out furniture: despawned without freeing its index, and the index retired so nothing later takes it. The death record above is what remains.

Before the entity goes, everything that points at it is released: the object it had reserved, its walk and interaction, a conversation it was in (including the other person's side), queued orders from others that name it, and the selection. The reservation code already records three places that would leak once removal exists; the first slice closes each of them with a test.

## [DE-body] No body, no ghost, no art

A dead sim simply stops being drawn. There is no body to step over and no ghost to meet: both need art and both belong to the ghost milestone (M4, Ghosts, in `FEATURES.md`). The need is logged as [T-death-art].

## [DE-consent] The player says whether this happens at all

A game about ordinary life that kills somebody by surprise has broken a promise. Death is **off by default** in this slice, behind one setting in Options: "Sims can die if hunger or energy stays empty". Turning it on says plainly what it means. The setting is simulation state, changed by a command and saved, so a replay and a load agree about it.

With death off, the deprivation count still runs, but no warning appears and nobody dies. With it on, the warning appears when the count says it should, and the death follows if nothing changes.
