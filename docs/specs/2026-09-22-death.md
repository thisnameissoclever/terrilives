# Death: the game is called Natural Causes

[B-death] in `FEATURES.md`. Nobody can die. The title promises otherwise, the ghost layer waits on it, and the family ties that just shipped have no bereavement to hook into. This document splits the item and defines the first slice.

The whole of it is large: aging and causes that are not aging, warnings a player can act on, preventable and unavoidable deaths, the body and the ghost, what it does to the household and to everybody's relationships, inheritance, memorial state, and the content controls a player needs to say "not that kind of game, thank you". None of that fits one increment.

## [DE-cause] One cause, and it is the player's to prevent

The first slice has exactly one way to die: **neglect**. A sim whose energy or hunger sits at zero for `death_after_ticks` (`content/tuning.toml`) dies. Nothing else kills anybody: no age, no fire, no accident, no illness.

Neglect is the right first cause because it is the only one the player can already see coming. The needs panel shows the bar, the mood shows the misery, and the warning below is a third telling. A death the player could not have prevented is a different design problem and waits for a later slice.

## [DE-warn] A warning that is a fact, not a hint

At `death_warning_ticks` before a death would happen, the sim's row says so plainly: their name, what is at zero, and that they will die if it stays there. The wording is functional and not a joke, as an error message is: this is the surface where humour would cost the most.

The warning is derived, never stored. It is what the numbers already say, said out loud.

## [DE-consequence] What a death leaves

- **The household loses them.** They stop being selectable, their needs and mood stop, and the roster no longer lists them.
- **Their ties stay.** A dead parent is still a parent ([FM-tie]), which is what the family tree and the bereavement hooks will read.
- **Everybody who knew them feels it.** Each surviving member's relationship toward them is kept, and a grief moodlet lands on every member of the household, its size scaled by that relationship. Grief decays like any other moodlet.
- **Their things stay theirs until inheritance exists.** Furniture they bought stays where it is and keeps working. Nothing is sold, moved or reassigned by this slice.
- **The save carries it.** A dead sim is saved as dead rather than deleted, because a deleted sim is one whose ties, grief and memorial have nowhere to live, and because a save must not silently lose a person.

## [DE-body] No body, no ghost, no art

A dead sim simply stops being drawn. There is no body to step over and no ghost to meet: both need art and both are the ghost layer's business ([B-ghosts] and [M4]). The need is logged as [T-death-art].

## [DE-consent] The player says whether this happens at all

A game about ordinary life that kills somebody by surprise has broken a promise. Death is **off by default** in this slice, behind one setting in Options: "Sims can die of neglect". Turning it on says plainly what it means. Nothing about the warning, the grief or the save changes with the setting; only whether the death itself happens.

## Slices

1. **[DE-slice-neglect]**, this one: the cause, the warning, the setting, the consequences above, and the save.
2. **[DE-slice-age]**: age as a cause, which needs ages, which nothing has yet.
3. **[DE-slice-ghost]**: the body, the ghost and the memorial, with the art they need.
4. **[DE-slice-inherit]**: what happens to what they owned, which needs [B-family-relationships]'s later slices to say who inherits.
