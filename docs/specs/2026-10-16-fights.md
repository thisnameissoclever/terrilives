# Fights

Issue 248 defines this change. Conversations, topics, waiting and the chore
board remain the owners of everything else about company and housework.

## Intended behavior

A player can select a person, right-click another person and choose Fight.
The two meet as a conversation does and argue instead of chatting: both
bubbles show a red topic, nobody's Social refills, and when it ends both
feel worse about each other and carry a Had a fight moodlet for a while.

People also fight on their own, but only with someone they can barely
stand, and only with a reason: a chore the other person owns was missed
and the mess is still there. A fight over the dishes shows a dish topic; a
fight over any other chore shows a cleaning topic; a fight the player
started with no such reason shows a plain argument.

## Rules

1. **Vocabulary.** `content/social.toml` gains a second interaction,
   `fight`, labelled Fight, tagged `fighting`, advertising nothing, with
   the chat's talk presentation and duration. It is the second row of the
   right-click menu over a person and index 1 of `SimCommand::TalkTo`. A
   social interaction is a fight when its tags hold `fighting`.
2. **Never chosen by need.** Ordinary selection skips fight interactions:
   they advertise nothing, and the people loop in `select_action` does
   not score them at all, so no need level can make one attractive.
3. **Autonomous fights.** Each tick, in id order, an idle person (no
   target, path, conversation, chain, meal, work or commute, and an empty
   order queue) with no running Had a fight moodlet looks for a reason
   against each other living person on the lot, in id order. The reason
   holds when the initiator's feeling toward the other is at or below
   `fight_affinity_threshold` (shipped -0.9, the bottom five percent of
   the -1 to 1 scale) and the chore board holds a settled Missed episode
   owned by the other person whose chore still needs work now. With a
   reason, one seeded draw against `fight_chance_per_tick` (shipped 0.01)
   decides whether a Fight order for that person is pushed to the front
   of the initiator's queue this tick; the first reason that passes wins
   and the search stops. The order is then served, waited on and started
   exactly as a player's Fight order is, with one difference: if the other
   person leaves the lot (for work or a commute) or dies before the
   initiator sets off, the order is withdrawn and the initiator freed,
   where a player's order would wait for them to come home. Only the queue
   entry the trigger pushed is watched: a player's order put ahead of it
   ends the watch, and the made-up order then waits as a player's would.
   The watched entries are not saved either, so after a load such an order
   waits as a player's would, at most until the other person returns.
4. **Topic.** When a fight starts, the initiator's topic is set from the
   reason rather than drawn: code 24 and tag `fight-dishes` for a missed
   Dishes episode, code 25 and tag `fight-chore` for any other missed
   chore, code 26 and tag `fight` when no reason exists (a player's
   order against someone whose chores are in hand). Both preferences are
   -1. A player-started fight uses the reason when one exists, so the
   bubble names what the fight is about. No draw is spent on a fight
   topic.
5. **Bubbles.** Both participants' bubbles draw the topic in a strong red
   tint, brighter than a disliked chat topic: the dish symbol for code
   24, the dish-washing symbol again for code 25 (no broom art is
   authored), and the speech symbol for code 26, all in their disapproval
   form.
6. **Delivery.** A fight fills no need on either side; the talk's
   presentation, voice clips and duration are unchanged. Completion pays
   no affinity gain, no hobby satisfaction and no practice. Instead each
   participant's feeling toward the other falls by
   `fight_relationship_loss` (shipped 0.1), recorded as a Fight
   relationship effect, and each records a Had a fight moodlet ending
   `now + fight_mood_ticks` (shipped 480, eight game hours) scoring
   `-fight_mood_penalty` (shipped 12). A second fight restarts the
   moodlet. An interrupted fight, like an interrupted chat, changes
   nothing. A running Had a fight moodlet also stops the person starting
   another autonomous fight, so a standing reason produces at most one
   fight per moodlet length.
7. **Save.** Schema twelve appends the fight rows to the schema-eleven
   envelope: one row per person, sorted by id, naming a living person,
   with an end tick no further ahead than the longest moodlet any tuning
   allows (10080 ticks). A dead person's row goes with them. A schema-
   eleven payload still loads, with no fights, and the browser keeps an
   untouched schema-eleven recovery copy the first time it writes schema
   twelve over one. Rows naming nobody living, repeating a person, out of
   order or ending too far ahead refuse the whole load. The list enters
   the world hash whenever it holds anything. A saved fight topic (code
   24 to 26, its matching tag, both preferences -1) is valid only on an
   initiator whose running interaction is a fight; a saved chat topic
   remains invalid on a fight.
8. **Tuning.** `fight_affinity_threshold`, `fight_chance_per_tick`,
   `fight_relationship_loss`, `fight_mood_penalty` and
   `fight_mood_ticks` are root knobs in `content/tuning.toml`, appended
   to the compiled tuning after `burnt_meal_mood_ticks`. The compiler
   refuses a threshold outside -1 to 1, a chance outside 0 to 1, a
   negative loss or loss above 2, a negative penalty, or a duration
   outside 1 to 10080 ticks. Packs published before the knobs existed
   reconstruct the shipped values.

## What this does not do

Fights do not change the chore board, the mess, who does the chore next,
or the missed episode's record. Nobody is hurt, nothing breaks, and there
is no shouting audio: the chat voice clips play. There is no event feed
entry; the feed does not exist yet.

## Verification

Unit tests pin the reason test (threshold edge, missed episode ownership,
work still needed, idle initiator, the moodlet cooldown), the one-draw
autonomous trigger across seeds, the withdrawal of a made-up order when the
other person leaves for work, the fight topic codes and tags, and the
compile ranges. Social tests drive a player Fight order to completion
(relationship loss on both sides, moodlets on both, no Social delivered,
no gain) and an interrupted fight (nothing recorded). Save tests round-
trip schema twelve with the digest unchanged, load schema eleven with no
fights, drop a dead person's row, refuse bad rows, and accept a fight
topic only on a fight. A wasm boundary test checks the public envelope
writes schema twelve and reads both, and the social vocabulary reports
Chat and Fight in order. Browser tests cover the three fight codes'
artwork and the strong red tint, and the Fight menu row.
