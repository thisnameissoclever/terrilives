## [SEA-year] The year is derived from the clock

A day is `day_ticks` ticks (1440) and day index `d` is `tick / day_ticks`, the number the HUD shows as "Day d + 1". The year has four seasons in a fixed order: Spring (0), Summer (1), Autumn (2) and Winter (3). Each lasts `days_per_season` days, a tuning number of at least 1; the shipped value is 7, so a year is 28 days and days 1, 8, 15 and 22 begin the four seasons. The season is `(d / days_per_season + first_season) % 4`, where `first_season` is a tuning number from 0 (Spring) to 3 (Winter) naming the season of day 1; the shipped value is 0, so a new game starts in Spring.

Nothing is saved and the world hash does not change: the tick is already saved and hashed, and the season is a pure function of it and content. `terri_core::clock::season` sits beside `weekday` in `crates/terri-core/src/clock.rs`, so the boundary, and any simulation rule that later reads the season, share one definition. `season` reduces the day count before adding the offset, so a one-tick day at the top of the tick range cannot overflow. The content compiler refuses a `days_per_season` of 0 and a `first_season` above 3. Changing either number can move a saved game to a different season, as changing `first_weekday` moves it to a different weekday.

## [SEA-hud] What the player reads

The household clock has three lines: `Day {n}, {Weekday}`, `{hh}:{mm}`, then `{Season}: {condition}`. For example, `Day 22, Monday`, `10:00` and `Winter: snowing` appear on separate lines. The simulation supplies the weekday and season. A bad weekday or season shows `Day and time unavailable`.

## [SEA-yard] The yard's grass changes color with the season

The yard's grass is drawn under the lot's yard look ([OS-yard] in `2026-09-22-the-outside.md`). Each season adds a color change of its own, with a hue, a strength and a lightness like a lot look, from `season_yard_looks` in `content/tuning.toml`. The file gives each season one table, keyed `spring`, `summer`, `autumn` and `winter`, for example `season_yard_looks.summer = { hue = -3.0, strength = 0.98, lightness = 0.015 }`. The compiled tuning holds them as four `[hue, strength, lightness]` rows in season order. The shell composes the current season's change with the yard look so that the one shift the shader applies equals the two applied one after the other in OKLab: the hue turns add, the color strengths multiply and the lightness shifts add. The equality is exact while nothing is clamped; the shader clamps lightness to 0 to 1 and the converted color to the displayable range, so where a single pass would have clipped, the composed shift and two passes differ.

| Season | Change | Intended look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | `[0, 1, 0]` | The grass as authored |
| Summer | `[-3, 0.98, 0.015]` | Slightly warmer green |
| Autumn | `[-6, 0.97, -0.005]` | Slightly warmer and darker green |
| Winter | `[3, 0.92, 0.025]` | Slightly cooler and lighter green |

A negative hue turn moves green toward yellow. The compiler holds each season's look to a lot look's ranges: hue -180 to 180 degrees, strength 0 to 2 and lightness -0.25 to 0.25. The tuning reader refuses a malformed table with the season named: a season missing, a key that is not one of the four seasons, a season written as anything but a table, a look missing a key or carrying an unknown one, and a value that is not a number. The house floors, the street and any floor covering laid on the yard keep their looks in every season.

The floor and walls are a static block of GPU instances, rebuilt whenever the shell runs `applyCamera`: through the camera-dirty gate in the frame loop, when the build actions surface pans the camera to keep the selection in view, and when newly loaded floor materials are published. Each frame the shell asks the simulation for the season and marks the static block dirty only when it differs from the last season it saw, so a season turn or a Load that lands in another season costs one rebuild, and an ordinary frame costs none. Every rebuild, whichever caller runs it, draws the yard in the last season the frame loop saw. A change seen while the floor is hidden or graphics are recovering keeps the block dirty until the next rebuild.

## [SEA-scope] What the season does not change

Seasons and weather have no gameplay effect. Needs, moods, careers, autonomy and saves are unchanged. Temperature effects, seasonal events and holidays, growing plants, seasonal moods, dates and months remain later work.

## [SEA-weather] Occasional visual weather

The owner approved visual weather on 2026-10-18. Most periods are mild. Each Summer day has a 35% chance of a hot period, each Autumn day a 40% chance of breezy leaf fall, and each Winter day a 20% chance of snow. Spring stays mild. Events last two game hours and begin at a variable time between 07:00 and 17:00. Independent daily hashes produce repeatable variation; they never consume household random draws. The season comes from the simulation, and durations scale to the authored day length.

Weather tuning lives in `[weather]` in `content/tuning.toml`: `seed`, four `event_day_chance` values in season order, `event_hours`, `earliest_hour`, `latest_hour`, `leaf_ground_hours` and `fall_minutes`. Validation requires finite probabilities in [0, 1], zero Spring probability, positive durations, ordered start times, a fall no longer than the event, and every leaf expired before midnight. The optional field follows the existing compiled tuning fields. Historical sources without it retain no weather effects; their immutable pack witnesses are preserved.

Snow falls only during its brief event and does not accumulate. Autumn leaves flutter down over fifteen game minutes and remain on the ground for two game hours after landing, fading during the last quarter of that lifetime. A bounded particle sampler derives positions and age from the game clock. Pause freezes it, speed changes advance it with the clock, and loading reconstructs it without adding saved fields. Leaves retain their positions on surviving outdoor tiles when the lot changes.

The Outside room map restricts effects to outdoor tiles, including the street. A second mask clips the projected footprint so elevated particles cannot paint over the cutaway interior. The effects pass writes color without changing scene ownership or depth. Reduced motion suppresses particles while preserving the weather text. The grass remains recognizably green throughout all four seasons.

## [SEA-tuning] Where the numbers live

Every number above is in `content/tuning.toml`, beside `day_ticks` and `first_weekday`. The three knobs are appended to the compiled tuning after `career_mood_fired_ticks`, in the order `days_per_season`, `first_season`, `season_yard_looks`. Packs published before them, and the frozen pre-books content sources, reconstruct the shipped values, so they decode to the same tuning as before plus the shipped seasons.

The yard looks are tuning numbers rather than a field beside the yard look in `content/lot.toml` for two reasons. The single-tuning-file rule puts every value a designer tunes for the whole system in one file, and the seasonal change applies to any lot's yard look rather than describing one lot. And a new lot field would have changed the compiled lot record, which every published pack witness and frozen lot source would then have had to reconstruct, where an appended tuning field already has that path.

## [SEA-rulings] Decisions for the owner to confirm

The calendar defaults were chosen during the original seasons work. The owner revised the visual presentation and approved weather on 2026-10-18.

1. Four seasons in a fixed order, Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter. Cost if wrong: a code and pack-layout change, because the count is fixed in `terri_core::clock::SEASON_COUNT`, in the four-row `season_yard_looks` field of the compiled tuning (and so in the published-pack reconstruction), and in the season names the compiler and the shell use.
2. Each season lasts seven days, so a year is four weeks. Cost if wrong: one tuning number, `days_per_season`.
3. A new game starts in Spring. Cost if wrong: one tuning number, `first_season`.
4. The owner requested separate date, time and season/weather lines on 2026-10-18. The day of the season is not shown.
5. The owner requested green grass with subtle seasonal tints on 2026-10-18. House floors, the street and floor coverings keep their colors.
6. The seasonal looks live in `content/tuning.toml`, not in `content/lot.toml`, for the reasons in [SEA-tuning]. Cost if wrong: moving the field and its compatibility reconstruction.
7. The owner approved visual weather without effects on needs or chores on 2026-10-18.
8. A season index the shell does not recognize hides the whole clock behind `Day and time unavailable`, matching a bad weekday, rather than showing the day and time without a season. Cost if wrong: a shell change.

## [SEA-evidence] Proof required before delivery

1. Season arithmetic in `crates/terri-core/src/clock.rs`: the first and last tick of every season of the shipped year and the turn into the second year; `first_season` 0 and 3; `days_per_season` 1 and 7; a 100-tick day; and the top of the tick range for a 1440-tick day, a one-tick day with one-day seasons, and the longest season.
2. Compile: a `days_per_season` of 0 is refused and 1 and `u32::MAX` accepted; a `first_season` of 4 is refused and 3 accepted; each look field just past its range, and a NaN, is refused with the season and field named, and the range edges are accepted; the shipped pack carries 7, 0 and the four looks in the table above. The tuning reader takes the seasons and keys by name, in any order and in the dotted-key or table-header layout, and accepts whole numbers; it refuses a fifth season, a season the year does not have, each missing season, each missing key, an unknown or fourth key, a season or the whole table written as a list of numbers, a value that is not a number, and a TOML date or time in place of the whole table, a season's look or a number, each naming the season and the key wherever one is at fault. A complete table in the table-header layout parses to the same rows as the inline one.
3. Pack layout: the season fields retain their existing positions before the later autonomy, meal-appreciation and weather fields. 4. Determinism: no saved field is added; reading the season at the boundary changes neither the save bytes nor the world hash; the full Rust and web suites pass with no other pinned value moving.
5.
