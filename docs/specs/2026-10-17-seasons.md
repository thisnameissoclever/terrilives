## [SEA-year] The year is derived from the clock

A day is `day_ticks` ticks (1440) and day index `d` is `tick / day_ticks`, the number the HUD shows as "Day d + 1". The year has four seasons in a fixed order: Spring (0), Summer (1), Autumn (2) and Winter (3). Each lasts `days_per_season` days, a tuning number of at least 1; the shipped value is 7, so a year is 28 days and days 1, 8, 15 and 22 begin the four seasons. The season is `(d / days_per_season + first_season) % 4`, where `first_season` is a tuning number from 0 (Spring) to 3 (Winter) naming the season of day 1; the shipped value is 0, so a new game starts in Spring. The day of the season is `d % days_per_season + 1`.

Nothing is saved and the world hash does not change: the tick is already saved and hashed, and the season is a pure function of it and content. `terri_core::clock::season` and `terri_core::clock::day_of_season` sit beside `weekday` in `crates/terri-core/src/clock.rs`, so the boundary, and any simulation rule that later reads the season, share one definition. `season` reduces the day count before adding the offset, so a one-tick day at the top of the tick range cannot overflow, and `day_of_season` cannot pass `u32::MAX`. The content compiler refuses a `days_per_season` of 0 and a `first_season` above 3. Changing either number can move a saved game to a different season, as changing `first_weekday` moves it to a different weekday.

## [SEA-hud] What the player reads

The household clock keeps the two lines [CAL-hud] gave it. The first still reads `Day {n}, {Weekday}`; the second reads `{Season}, {hh}:{mm}`, for example `Day 1, Monday` above `Spring, 06:00`. The season names are plain words written in the shell, as the weekday names are. Each has six letters, so the second line has thirteen characters all year, fewer than the longest first lines the panels already hold, such as `Day 10, Wednesday`; neither the narrow desktop panel nor the compact phone panel needs more width. The season comes from the simulation (`season_index` across the boundary); the shell never works one out from the day number. A season index outside 0 to 3 shows `Day and time unavailable`, as a bad weekday does. The day of the season is not shown.

## [SEA-yard] The yard's grass changes color with the season

The yard's grass is drawn under the lot's yard look ([OS-yard] in `2026-09-22-the-outside.md`). Each season adds a color change of its own, `[hue, strength, lightness]` like a lot look, from `season_yard_looks` in `content/tuning.toml`. The shell composes the current season's change with the yard look so that the one shift the shader applies equals the two applied one after the other in OKLab: the hue turns add, the color strengths multiply and the lightness shifts add. The equality is exact while nothing is clamped; the shader clamps lightness to 0 to 1 and the converted color to the displayable range, so where a single pass would have clipped, the composed shift and two passes differ.

| Season | Change | Intended look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | `[0, 1, 0]` | The grass as authored |
| Summer | `[-6, 0.9, 0.02]` | A little warmer, drier and lighter |
| Autumn | `[-45, 0.9, -0.05]` | A yellow-brown cast |
| Winter | `[12, 0.3, 0.14]` | Pale and desaturated, with a frosty lightness |

A negative hue turn moves green toward yellow. The compiler holds each row to a lot look's ranges: hue -180 to 180 degrees, strength 0 to 2 and lightness -0.25 to 0.25. The house floors, the street and any floor covering laid on the yard keep their looks in every season.

The floor and walls are a static block of GPU instances, rebuilt only through the shell's camera-dirty gate. Each frame the shell asks the simulation for the season and marks the static block dirty only when it differs from the last season it saw, so the block is rebuilt once when a season turns, once when a Load lands in another season, and never on an ordinary frame. A change seen while the floor is hidden or graphics are recovering keeps the block dirty until the next rebuild.

## [SEA-scope] What the season does not change

This slice has no gameplay effect. Needs, moods, careers, autonomy and saves are unchanged, and nothing in the simulation reads the season. Weather, temperature, seasonal events and holidays, plants that grow or wither, seasonal moods, and dates and months are later work.

## [SEA-tuning] Where the numbers live

Every number above is in `content/tuning.toml`, beside `day_ticks` and `first_weekday`. The three knobs are appended to the compiled tuning after `career_mood_fired_ticks`, in the order `days_per_season`, `first_season`, `season_yard_looks`. Packs published before them, and the frozen pre-books content sources, reconstruct the shipped values, so they decode to the same tuning as before plus the shipped seasons.

The yard looks are tuning numbers rather than a field beside the yard look in `content/lot.toml` for two reasons. The single-tuning-file rule puts every value a designer tunes for the whole system in one file, and the seasonal change applies to any lot's yard look rather than describing one lot. And a new lot field would have changed the compiled lot record, which every published pack witness and frozen lot source would then have had to reconstruct, where an appended tuning field already has that path.

## [SEA-rulings] Decisions for the owner to confirm

Each was decided during autonomous work on 2026-10-17, without the owner, and is recorded here for the owner to confirm or change. The cost if wrong is given for each.

1. Four seasons in a fixed order, Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter. Cost if wrong: a code and pack-layout change, because the count is fixed in `terri_core::clock::SEASON_COUNT`, in the four-row `season_yard_looks` field of the compiled tuning (and so in the published-pack reconstruction), and in the season names the compiler and the shell use.
2. Each season lasts seven days, so a year is four weeks. Cost if wrong: one tuning number, `days_per_season`.
3. A new game starts in Spring. Cost if wrong: one tuning number, `first_season`.
4. The clock shows the season before the time on its second line, and does not show the day of the season. Cost if wrong: a shell change to `formatSimTime`.
5. The only visible change is the yard grass's tint, with the four values in [SEA-yard]; the house floors, the street and floor coverings do not change. Cost if wrong: four tuning rows for the values; a code change to tint anything else.
6. The seasonal looks live in `content/tuning.toml`, not in `content/lot.toml`, for the reasons in [SEA-tuning]. Cost if wrong: moving the field and its compatibility reconstruction.
7. The season has no gameplay effect in this slice. Cost if wrong: a later slice.
8. A season index the shell does not recognize hides the whole clock behind `Day and time unavailable`, matching a bad weekday, rather than showing the day and time without a season. Cost if wrong: a shell change.

## [SEA-evidence] Proof required before delivery

1. Season arithmetic in `crates/terri-core/src/clock.rs`: the first and last tick of every season of the shipped year and the turn into the second year; `first_season` 0 and 3; `days_per_season` 1 and 7; a 100-tick day; and the top of the tick range for a 1440-tick day, a one-tick day with one-day seasons, and the longest season.
2. Compile: a `days_per_season` of 0 is refused and 1 and `u32::MAX` accepted; a `first_season` of 4 is refused and 3 accepted; each look field just past its range, and a NaN, is refused with the season and field named, and the range edges are accepted; the shipped pack carries 7, 0 and the four rows in the table above.
3. Pack layout: the three knobs are the final fifty bytes of the serialized tuning and every earlier slot is unchanged; the frozen published witnesses still decode byte for byte and convert to the live layout; the schema refuses a tuning file missing any of the three; the inherited runtime fixture grows by exactly the fifty season bytes.
4. Determinism: no saved field is added; reading the season at the boundary changes neither the save bytes nor the world hash; the full Rust and web suites pass with no other pinned value moving.
5. Boundary and HUD: `season_index` starts in Spring, turns on the first tick of days 8, 15, 22 and 29, applies a retuned season length and first season, and names a season at the top of the tick range; `season_yard_looks` returns the four rows in season order; the clock text renders each season; the yard look in each season, on both the historical sprite path and the accepted-grass path, with the house and the street unchanged; the static block is marked dirty only when the season changes.
6. Each guard above fails its tests when deliberately broken: the season offset, the season length, the reduction before the offset, the day-of-season count, the compile check, the first-season bound, both boundary tuning reads, the clock's season, the season name check, the yard composition, the strength product, the redraw check and the bridge read.
7. A played check at desktop and phone widths shows the new clock text and the yard grass in all four seasons, with screenshots, and confirms the grass pattern stays readable in each.
