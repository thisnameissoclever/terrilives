# Careers: paths, reviews and autonomy

Issue 249 defines career paths and reviews. The calendar's working days stay as
[CAL-careers] in `2026-10-06-calendar.md` defines them. [Player orders and
attendance](2026-10-17-player-orders.md) supersede the older work-priority and
arrival-based shift-duration rules: player orders take priority, each shift ends
at its original scheduled time, and pay and performance reflect actual attendance.

## Intended behavior

Every job sits on a career path with titled levels, each level with its own hours, working days, pay range, need cost and expectations. A person can be given a job by the player or can find one alone; an unemployed person looks for work, and an employed person whose job suits them badly may change path. Every shift is scored from how well the job fits the person's traits and social capacity, their relevant skill and their mood on leaving. At the end of each in-game week every employed person is reviewed: the week's score decides, with a limited random part, whether nothing happens, they are promoted (which always raises pay), given a raise within their job, demoted, or, after at least three poor weeks in a row, fired. The player reads the path, the title, the pay and its range, the schedule, the week's score so far, the factors behind it, what the next promotion needs, warnings, and a graph of past weeks with the events marked. Two settings, both on by default, let people change career and look for work on their own: changing career is one household setting, and job search is each person's own setting, defined in [job search for each housemate](2026-10-18-job-search-per-person.md). The reviews run whatever the settings say.

## Delivery

The simulation side shipped first: rules 1 to 14, 16, 17 and 19, with the commands of rule 12 and the settings of rule 15 reachable by the runtime. The Career tab, the Overview row's trend and information control, and the Options checkboxes (rules 15 and 18) with their browser tests ship in the second pull request.

## Rules

1. **Paths and levels.** `content/careers.toml` holds `[[career]]` paths, each with an `id`, a `label`, a `social_demand` from 0 to 1 (how far the work faces other people), an optional `skill` (a skill tag the work reads), a `traits` table of trait id to fit weight from -1 to 1, and an ordered list of `[[career.level]]` entries. A level has an `id`, a `title`, `shift_start`, `shift_ticks`, `pay` (the starting pay of the level, whole dollars per shift), `pay_max` (the most the level pays), `energy_cost`, `satisfaction` and `working_days`, exactly as a career did before this change, plus `advance_weeks`, the number of good weeks in a row a promotion out of the level needs. The compiler keeps the flat compiled career list, one row per level, so a `Career(u32)` component and a saved career id keep meaning one job; every row gains its path index, its level index, its `pay_max`, its `advance_weeks`, the path's social demand, skill tag and trait fit list. The compiler refuses a path with no levels, a level whose `pay_max` is below its `pay`, a level whose `pay_max` is not above the previous level's `pay_max`, a level whose `pay` is below the previous level's `pay`, an unknown trait or skill, a fit weight outside -1 to 1, a social demand outside 0 to 1, a duplicate level id across every path (path ids and level ids share one id space, so a level may not reuse a path's id either), and `advance_weeks` of 0 or above 52. `advance_weeks` is required, like `working_days`. The shipped office job keeps the level id `office_job`, so every save that names it still loads. [Wages and bills](2026-10-18-wages-and-bills.md) raised every level's `pay` and `pay_max` to 1.5 times their values here, rounded half up, and adds a night premium ([WB-wages]).
2. **Shipped content.** Three paths ship, each with four levels: the office path the shipped worker already holds (clerk upward, Monday to Friday mornings), a cafe path that faces the public (barista upward, early starts including the weekend, social demand high, the cooking skill), and a night watch path (quiet night shifts, social demand near zero, low pay that climbs slowly, the exercise skill). Titles may be dry but must name the job; the owner reviews them before release per [L58]. [shifts and more career paths](2026-10-18-shifts-and-careers.md) adds five paths, gives every level a standard shift and lets a path offer other shifts by weight ([WS-model], [WS-content]). The shipped pay of every level is set by [wages and bills](2026-10-18-wages-and-bills.md) ([WB-wages]).
3. **Social capacity.** A person's social capacity is `1 - shyness / 100`, plus 0.2 for the `chatterbox` disposition, minus 0.2 for `private_person`, minus 0.3 for the `isolated` condition while worn, clamped to 0 to 1. It is not the Social need meter. The social gap of a job is `max(0, social_demand - capacity)`.
4. **Fit.** A job's fit for a person is `clamp(trait_fit - 2 * social_gap, -1, 1)`, where `trait_fit` is the sum of the path's fit weights over the traits the person wears. Fit is read whenever a shift is scored, a job is chosen or applied for, and the Career section is drawn; it is never stored.
5. **Shift score.** When a shift ends (the return that pays), the shift scores `50 + 20 * fit + 20 * (mastery - 0.5) + 10 * mood`, clamped to 0 to 100, where `mastery` is the person's mastery in the path's skill (0.5 when the path names none) and `mood` is the person's overall mood score at that moment divided by 50 and clamped to -1 to 1. The score joins the person's current week. A shift that started in one week and ends in the next counts in the week it ends.
6. **Weekly review.** The week is `day / 7` as the chore board counts it. On the first tick of a new week, every employed person who completed at least one shift in the week that ended is reviewed, in sim id order; a person at work or commuting on that tick is reviewed instead when they come home: at the shift's end, after it is paid and before its score joins the new week, or at the end of the walk home when the week ended during the walk, so no outcome changes a job while its shift is being worked. The week's performance is the mean of its shift scores plus a draw from the careers stream uniform in `-career_review_noise` to `+career_review_noise`, clamped to 0 to 100; a person with no completed shift that week is not reviewed and their counters do not move. A week is poor below `career_poor_threshold` and good at or above `career_good_threshold`; between them it is neither. Consecutive poor weeks and consecutive good weeks are counted and reset by any week of the other kind or of neither.
7. **Outcomes.** One draw from the careers stream decides at most one outcome per review, tested in this order with these chances, each clamped to 0 to 1 and read from tuning:
   - firing: 0 until the review is the third consecutive poor week or later, then `career_fire_base * (consecutive_poor - 2)`;
   - demotion, only above the path's first level: `career_demote_base * (poor_threshold - performance) / poor_threshold` when the week is poor, else 0;
   - promotion, only below the path's last level and when the consecutive good weeks reach the level's `advance_weeks`: `career_promote_base * (performance - 50) / 50` when performance is above 50, else 0;
   - raise, only while pay is below the level's `pay_max`: `career_raise_base * (performance - 50) / 50` when performance is above 50, else 0.
   The chances are summed in that order and the draw picks the first whose cumulative sum it falls under. The shipped bases are tuned so that a person who performs at the middle of the good range sees about one event every four to six weeks across the four outcomes together; the balance test in Verification measures it.
8. **What an outcome does.** A promotion moves the person to the next level and sets their pay to the larger of that level's `pay` and their old pay plus one dollar, never above the new level's `pay_max`; it is one event and it always raises pay. A raise adds a quarter of the level's range, rounded up and at least one dollar, never above `pay_max`. A demotion moves the person down a level and clamps their pay into that level's range. Firing removes the career and its record; the person is unemployed and the history starts again with the next job. Every outcome is recorded against the week with its kind; a review with no outcome records the week alone. Consecutive counters reset after a promotion, a demotion or a firing. A night shift pays the record's pay plus the night premium of [wages and bills](2026-10-18-wages-and-bills.md) ([WB-wages]); the outcomes here change the record's pay only.
9. **Warnings.** From the second consecutive poor week the person carries a warning: a Warned at work moodlet while the streak lasts, and the Career section says dismissal becomes possible from the third poor week in a row. A review resets the warning with the streak.
10. **Job satisfaction.** Separately from the score, each person holds a job satisfaction from 0 to 100, starting at 50 when hired, that moves after every shift one `career_satisfaction_rate` step toward `50 + 40 * fit`. Below `career_job_misery` it adds a Hates the job moodlet and debits `career_misery_satisfaction` life satisfaction points per completed shift on top of the level's authored reward; above `career_job_joy` it adds an Enjoys the job moodlet. A person can score well at a job they hate: the score reads fit, mastery and mood, not job satisfaction.
11. **Event moodlets.** Promoted and Got a raise are positive moodlets lasting `career_mood_promoted_ticks` (shipped one day); Demoted lasts `career_mood_demoted_ticks` (two days) and Fired `career_mood_fired_ticks` (four days), both negative; the amounts are tuning. They are timed like Had a fight and saved the same way.
12. **Hiring on direction.** `SimCommand::AssignCareer { agent, path }` puts the person at the path's first level at its starting pay, replacing any job, with fresh counters and job satisfaction 50; `SimCommand::QuitCareer { agent }` makes them unemployed. Both are refused for a person at work or commuting, for an unknown path, and for anyone who is not a living person. The result is readable by the shell, with a count of the career commands the drain has handled, applied or refused, which rises by one with each; the settings command of rule 15 is not counted. An assignment draws the shift the person holds from the level's weights; `SimCommand::AssignCareerShift` assigns with a chosen shift and `SimCommand::ChangeShift` changes an employed person's shift, as [shifts and more career paths](2026-10-18-shifts-and-careers.md) defines ([WS-choose], [WS-select]).
13. **Autonomous search.** The `Careers` resource holds `autonomous_change`, true in a new game and in every save from before careers, and each person's job search setting as [job search for each housemate](2026-10-18-job-search-per-person.md) defines it. An unemployed person whose search is on looks for work once per working day, Monday to Friday, when the day's tick equals `career_search_tick` taken modulo the day's length: with probability `career_search_chance` they apply, choose a path by a draw weighted by fit ([WS-search] in [shifts and more career paths](2026-10-18-shifts-and-careers.md); a search temperature of 0 keeps the best fit, ties to the lower path index), and are hired at its first level with probability `clamp(0.5 + 0.5 * fit, 0.1, 0.95)`. Every draw comes from the careers stream, in sim id order.
14. **Autonomous change.** While `autonomous_change` is on, a review that ends with no outcome also asks whether the person changes path: when their fit is below `career_change_fit` and some other path's fit exceeds theirs by at least `career_change_gap`, they move to that path's first level with probability `career_change_chance`, keeping nothing of their seniority. A change is recorded against the week as its own event kind and counts as the review's one outcome.
15. **Settings.** The Options flyout shows Allow sims to change careers autonomously, a household setting. Job search is each person's own setting, set in the Career tab, as [job search for each housemate](2026-10-18-job-search-per-person.md) defines it together with the commands and saves; the older `SimCommand::SetCareerAutonomy { change, search }` survives only for saves that hold it queued. Turning either off never stops player-directed assignment or the weekly reviews.
16. **Save.** Schema fourteen appends to the schema-thirteen envelope: the settings, the careers stream, one record per employed person with their pay, counters, job satisfaction, the current week's shift scores, the history of reviewed weeks (week, performance, event kind) and the week whose review waits for their return, the event moodlet timers, and the count of handled career commands. A death drops the person's record and timers with their other records. A record naming a person who is not alive, a pay outside their level's range, a history out of week order, a score outside 0 to 100, a week ahead of the clock, a waiting review for a person at home, with nothing scored, or for a week already reviewed or still running, or the same moodlet twice refuses the load. A save from schema thirteen or earlier loads with the settings on, an empty history, pay at each level's starting pay and job satisfaction 50. The settings, the stream, the command count and every record enter the world hash, so a quit that leaves no other trace still changes it.
17. **Tuning.** Every number above is a root knob in `content/tuning.toml`, appended after `land_price_step_cents` in this order: `career_review_noise`, `career_poor_threshold`, `career_good_threshold`, `career_fire_base`, `career_demote_base`, `career_promote_base`, `career_raise_base`, `career_search_tick`, `career_search_chance`, `career_change_fit`, `career_change_gap`, `career_change_chance`, `career_satisfaction_rate`, `career_job_misery`, `career_job_joy`, `career_misery_satisfaction`, `career_mood_joy`, `career_mood_misery`, `career_mood_warned`, `career_mood_promoted`, `career_mood_raise`, `career_mood_demoted`, `career_mood_fired`, `career_mood_promoted_ticks`, `career_mood_demoted_ticks`, `career_mood_fired_ticks`. The compiler refuses thresholds outside 0 to 100 with poor below good and misery below joy, chances and bases outside 0 to 1, a review noise outside 0 to 50, a search tick above 10080 (a week of shipped days; the tick is read modulo the day, so a fixture with a shorter day keeps the shipped value), a change fit outside -1 to 1 or a gap outside 0 to 2, a satisfaction rate outside 0 to 100, negative mood amounts or misery cost, and moodlet ticks outside 1 to 10080.
18. **The shell.** Sim details gains a Career tab. It shows the path and the title, the level as n of m, the pay and the level's range, the schedule, this week's score so far, the last review's performance, the factors (trait fit, social capacity against the job's demand, skill mastery, mood on the last shift), the consecutive counters with what the next promotion needs, the warning when one stands, and a graph of the reviewed weeks with a green upward arrow at a promotion, a red downward arrow at a demotion, a green money mark at a raise and a gray diamond at a change of path, each titled with its week and event. Wherever the title appears it is green when the last three reviews were all good and red when they were all poor, and a small information control beside it opens a miniature of the same graph. The tab offers the paths to assign and a Quit control; the same commands the simulation defines, nothing else. The schedule names the held shift, and Shift selectors choose the shift an assignment holds and change an employed person's shift ([WS-shell] in [shifts and more career paths](2026-10-18-shifts-and-careers.md)).
19. **Two workers.** Two people whose shifts start on the same tick leave together: the sweep that cancels a walk toward a leaving worker skips a worker who is leaving on the same tick, so neither loses its commute.

## What this does not do

No workplace lot, no colleagues and no shift events. No sick days or holidays. No effort setting per shift. The character creator does not yet offer a career; a new housemate starts unemployed and the search rule finds them work. No career for children; there are none.

## Verification

Compiler tests refuse each bad path and level and compile the shipped three paths. Simulation tests score a shift from fit, mastery and mood; review a week and apply each outcome with the stream fixed; prove firing is impossible before the third consecutive poor week and grows after it; prove a promotion always raises pay and a raise stays inside the range; measure over two hundred reviewed weeks, with the person returned to the first level at its starting pay after each event so that an outcome stays on offer, that a middle-of-good performer sees between one event every four weeks and one every six across the four outcomes, that such a performer is never fired, that performance moves the chances and that the noise is bounded; hire and fire by command and refuse the refused cases; apply a review that fell due while a person was at work on their return, including a dismissal; drop a dead person's record so the next save still loads; run every combination of the two settings and show reviews continue with both off; move a poorly fitted person to a better path and leave a well fitted one; round-trip schema fourteen and load schema thirteen with the defaults; and run the shipped household for a week with every working day covered, measuring funds, needs and life satisfaction to show employment leaves time to live. Browser tests cover the Career tab's text, the graph's markers, the title colors and the information control, the Options checkboxes and the assignment controls.
