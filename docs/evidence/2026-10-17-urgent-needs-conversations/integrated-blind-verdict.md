## Findings and proof limits

1. **Actionable failures:** N retains the reported idle-motion loop; L loses the waiting partner's name on the narrow screen. These verdicts apply independently to those captured conditions. They do not establish which implementation produced any image.
2. **State is not animation playback:** A recorded `walk` or `talk` clip name confirms selected state, not rendered motion quality. The positional sequence provides evidence of route progress; the stride, gestures, and conversation bubbles provide limited visual corroboration. Continuous motion, foot sliding, transition smoothness, and intermittent flicker require observing the running game.
3. **UI refresh sampling:** The first M/R sample has `walk` with an `Idle` label, and the final sample at tick 77 has `idle` with a `Talking` label. The next early sample already has the walking label; there is no follow-up sample after the final transition. These observations show sampled state/label disagreement, but do not establish a persistent UI defect. Samples lack wall-clock timestamps and the UI refresh cadence, so the duration of either mismatch cannot be measured. Confirm the final label catches up on the next scheduled refresh before claiming transition labels are fully verified.
4. **Critical-needs outcome remains uncertain:** Critical hunger, social, and fun remain visible during the successful-looking interactions. The later `careTalk` values still include very low needs. This evidence proves selected route, wait, and conversation behavior at sampled times; it does not prove autonomous recovery of all critical needs, or exclude a later recurrence of the loop.
5. **Capture metadata is supporting evidence:** The empty error array and `browserClosed` field are recorded claims, not independently observed runtime checks by this review. No browser or server was operated.

**Next steps**: Use N and L as rejected conditions; verify the post-conversation label refresh and critical-needs recovery in the running game before treating those outcomes as accepted.
