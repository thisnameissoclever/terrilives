# Mood and personality

## Mood is how they feel now {#mood}

The dock shows the selected person's current mood: Miserable, Low, Okay, Good or Great. Open [Sim details](ui-elements/#sim-details), then Overview, to see its reasons. These reasons are moodlets. A positive number raises mood; a negative one lowers it.

Needs, conditions, nearby people, grief, dirt, dishes, waiting and recent events can all contribute. A pleasant activity does not guarantee a good overall mood when several other things are wrong. Read the named reasons before trying to fix the meter by repeating one favorite action.

Waiting for an occupied item lowers mood more when the activity could help a pressing need. Walking, working and talking are not the same waiting state. Removing dirt or dishes can improve mood without adding personal Hygiene, and nearby liked company can improve mood without adding Social.

Fresh mess has a grace period before ordinary mood penalties and complaints. Dishes blocking a seat are an immediate problem. See [mess and nuisance](relationships/#mess-and-nuisance) for the difference. A bad mood can make a person more likely to argue about an actual grievance; it does not invent someone to blame. See [arguments](relationships/#fights).

## Life satisfaction looks further ahead {#life-satisfaction}

Life satisfaction is a separate longer-term assessment. The dock shows its status and meter. Hover or keyboard-focus the meter for its exact score. Completed rewarding activities, hobbies, work, conditions, sustained mood and neglect can affect it.

A momentary good mood and a lasting improvement are different outcomes. Repeated bad days can reduce satisfaction, while keeping needs healthy and completing personally rewarding activities helps over time. A need benefit does not automatically mean a satisfaction reward, and an interrupted activity can retain minute-by-minute need benefits without earning its completion reward.

## Personality changes choice and need rates {#personality}

Open Overview, then Personality, habits and bed to read the selected person's pattern. Edit opens the [character creator](ui-elements/#creator) for changes. People can differ in how quickly each need falls, how strongly positive activities refill it, what they choose, sleep timing, cleanliness and shyness.

Activity preference affects how attractive an action is. A person avoiding television is not proof that television has no Fun benefit. Preferences, travel, repetition and urgent needs all influence the next autonomous choice. Your orders can direct an activity the person would rarely choose alone. Reading also applies reading preferences and disposition traits to its Fun reward; see [books and reading](activities/#reading).

Positive refill multipliers apply to the relevant need; negative costs are not discounted by them. Need-loss multipliers affect the ongoing loss of that meter. A high refill does not imply slow loss, and a favored activity does not manufacture a new benefit.

Shyness affects privacy respect and unwanted interruptions. Cleanliness affects tolerance for mess and cleanup decisions. Neither is a current need meter, and shyness alone does not determine compatibility with another person.

## Traits and conditions {#traits}

Choose [Traits](ui-elements/#traits) inside Sim details to see the person's traits and their explanations.

| Kind | What it changes | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disposition | Pull toward or avoidance of tagged activities | Usually changes choice; reading-related dispositions also scale reading Fun and satisfaction |
| Capability | Chance of a fumble linked to a skill | Completed attempts can teach even when they fail |
| Condition | Long-term satisfaction accrual and a changing severity | Suitable completed activities can help manage it |

Conditions can contribute mood penalties. Their severity is not a need deficit or a skill level. Capability mastery comes from its matching skill. Read the actual row rather than treating every trait percentage as the same kind of number.

## Managing conditions {#conditions}

The current conditions are Low spirits, Isolated and Cooped up. Choose a person, open [Traits](ui-elements/#traits), and read the named condition and its severity. A higher severity means a stronger effect. Conditions reduce the life-satisfaction rewards the person earns from activities and can add a negative mood reason. They do not directly drain a need meter.

| Condition | What to do | What must happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low spirits | Choose Handle correspondence at an available desk | The person must reach the desk and complete the correspondence activity; merely sitting there or starting it is insufficient |
| Isolated | Choose Chat with another person | Both people must meet and finish the chat; waiting for them or interrupting it does not count. Fight does not manage this condition |
| Cooped up | Choose Use the exercise bike on an available bike | The person must reach the bike and complete the exercise activity; simply going outdoors does not manage this condition |

Each completed matching activity reduces the condition's severity a little, toward zero. Management follows the activity, so its usual access, waiting and completion requirements still apply. Other activities can improve needs or mood without reducing this condition. Completing the matching activity does not remove the trait itself.

As severity falls, the condition reduces activity satisfaction less, and its mood penalty weakens. At zero severity it has no remaining penalty. A small residual severity may already be below the threshold for showing its mood reason. Conditions affect satisfaction from activities generally, not just the activity used to manage them; several active conditions can combine. See [correspondence and exercise](activities/#other-pastimes), [Chat](activities/#talk-and-fight), and [life satisfaction](#life-satisfaction).

These conditions also lower a new person's starting life satisfaction. Managing severity does not reset their score or undo that starting difference; it improves what the person can earn from activities going forward.

## Hobbies and skills {#hobbies-and-skills}

Hobbies identify kinds of activities the person finds particularly rewarding. Completing a loved activity increases its life-satisfaction reward. That does not add an extra Hunger, Energy or other need payment. Conditions can separately scale what the person earns.

Skills grow through completed attempts. Cooking, Fitness and Reading have their own progress. Capability-linked skill mastery lowers the chance of relevant mishaps, including cooking problems. Skills do not universally increase speed or every need benefit. An interrupted reading session preserves progress through the book but gives no completed skill attempt.

Open Overview, then [Skills](ui-elements/#skills) to see levels and progress. [Likes and dislikes](ui-elements/#likes-and-dislikes) describes personal responses to objects and surroundings; these are distinct from learned skill.

## Repetition and overdoing it {#repetition}

Repeating the same activity on the same object makes it less appealing to autonomous choice. This habituation fades over time. It does not reduce ordinary physical need-delivery rates.

Going beyond the usual repetition range can add an Overdoing moodlet. Excessive repeated eating can also cause Feeling sick. These affect mood and long-term satisfaction even if the action continues refilling its ordinary need. Give the activity time to become appealing again instead of expecting another identical use to clear the mood reason.

Books also have title-specific familiarity. A new physical copy does not reset a person's memory of the same book. A reading pass keeps its starting novelty through its sessions, so splitting a long title into sessions does not repeatedly create a first read.

## Likes, surroundings and other people {#likes-and-dislikes}

People can like or dislike object kinds. Some reactions concern an object present in the room; others concern active use. These can affect mood. Strong dislike of another person's TV or radio use in the same room can also reduce the observer's feeling toward the user.

Relationships create their own nearby mood effects. Compatibility is based on interests, dispositions and hobbies. It is not the same as current friendship, and changing a preference does not directly set a new opinion. See [relationship causes](relationships/#causes).

## Work and mood {#work}

The Career tab separates job performance from job satisfaction. Traits, social capacity, relevant skill and mood contribute to a shift's performance. A person can perform well at a job they dislike. Weekly reviews can bring raises, promotions, demotions, warnings or eventual dismissal.

Recent career events and an enjoyable or miserable job can add moodlets. The career graph shows reviewed weeks; a pending week is not a completed review. [Career controls](ui-elements/#career) show how to assign a path, quit or change that person's automatic job-search setting.

## Coming soon: creative pastimes {#coming-soon}

Gardening, knitting and painting are planned creative pastimes. Sims will gain personal satisfaction as they develop these hobbies and level up, including activities they do simply because they enjoy them. They are not current playable activities.

These hobbies will eventually produce things for the home, such as paintings, flowers and throw blankets. Some creations may be sold for money. Gardening may provide food for the fridge and reduce food spending. Exact level requirements, sale prices and food yields have not been decided, and there is no release date for these additions.
